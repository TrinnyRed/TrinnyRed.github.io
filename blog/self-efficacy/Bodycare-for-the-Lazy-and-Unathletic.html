Bodycare for the Lazy & Unathletic

Jul 22, 2026

You don’t even need equipment. Just an exercise mat, if that.

I’m not athletic.

So my minimum exercise intensity is, if I get tired via physical exertion, like, getting a little out-of-breath, that’s good enough. If my muscles start to shake, even better.

I’m also lazy. So my minimum routine is, keep it simple, but consistent.

As a lazy person, consistency is easier on my brain. “I’ll do x every day.” It’s a no-brainer. There’s no strategy. No schedule. Just do x every day, even if it’s not done well, and I’m good.

Easy & Simple Bodycare Checklist:

  1. Get 6-8 hours of sleep
  2. Finish at least 1 full water tumbler a day
  3. Consume 2000 calories worth of food*
  4. Eat a balanced diet
  5. Exercise regularly

1. Get 6-8 hours of sleep

Use sleeping aids if you have to. I use magnesium and melatonin every night. I’ve found the right pillow configuration for my back. I’ve invested $2000+ on a good mattress (Purple works for me). I cover my eyes with a dark navy sweater, and that helps block out light while I sleep.

2. Finish at least 1 full water tumbler a day

Don’t worry too much about the size. I can never remember how many ounces my tumbler is. Just finish whatever it is by end-of-day. (I just looked, and it’s 27 oz.)

3. Consume 2000 calories worth of food*

Your *Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is how many calories your body needs in a normal day to survive. Use an online calculator to get yours. Mine is around 1200-1500 calories based on my height, sex, and daily activity levels.

Calorie Count your food intake, and be in that ballpark range. Too much, and you gain weight. Too little, and you’ll feel like shit. Unless you’re trying to lose weight, aim for just about.

If you are overweight: err on the side of LESS calories.

If you are underweight: err on the side of MORE calories.

4. Eat a balanced diet

A “balanced diet” just means the nourishment you biologically need: protein, fiber, vitamins and minerals, carbohydrates.

My diet consists of oatmeal (carbs, fiber), beans (protein), eggs (protein), dates (vitamins, fiber), cheese (vitamins, protein); I don’t cook, so it’s pretty basic. These foods are microwavable and keep very well.

It’s ok to eat junk too.

Junk is defined as anything high in calories but poor in nutrition, meaning it doesn’t provide anything your cells and organs need to work well. That’s part of the fun in life. I snack on cookies and chocolates almost daily.

If you’re feeling hungry but you’ve hit your calorie limit of the day, you know the unlimited thing you can eat to stave off the hunger?

Fruits and veggies.

Eat as many fruits and veggies as you like if you’re hungry. I usually have bananas and mandarins, maybe some steamed broccoli (microwaved) or baby carrots. They fill you up fast, and your body doesn’t get that compulsion to binge on them like it does with salty, sugary, or fatty foods.

5. Exercise regularly

I do a modified One Punch Man routine:

  • 100 push-ups
  • 100 crunches
  • 100 squats
  • 100 opposite leg drives

What I lack in ability, I make up for with consistency.

I do it imperfectly daily, like I brush my teeth daily.

It took me about 6 years to gain enough strength and stamina to do the whole routine. So even if you can only do a quarter of it daily, keep going.

I don’t do any sports and was never athletic, but I try to do that much for my body.

Find a system you can do daily that works for you. Something you can keep doing until you’re really, really old.

Your body will thank you. 🦋
